Each dot is one project. The horizontal position is what the model expected it to cost; the vertical position is what it actually cost, adjusted to 2019 prices. Click a dot to open it in the explorer.

Expected costs are out-of-fold predictions: each project was predicted by a model that never saw it during training, so a project cannot pull its own prediction toward itself.

A dot above the line can also mean the model is missing something real, such as difficult terrain or right-of-way costs. That is why every flag goes to a person for review.

Method

From a procurement notice to a screening flag

  1. Extract

    Project notices are pulled by hand from PhilGEPS (Category + Area of Delivery = Albay). PhilGEPS has no public API.

  2. Quality check

    Each row gets a tier: COMPLETE, USABLE_PARTIAL, USABLE_SPARSE, or UNUSABLE. Paving area comes from the Bill of Quantities, with length × width from the plans as fallback.

  3. Prepare features

    Costs are adjusted to 2019 prices. Features: paving area, surface thickness, surface type, duration, year, and an ancillary-works flag.

  4. Predict expected cost

    XGBoost and a multiple linear regression baseline, both scored with 5-fold cross-validation. Both results are reported.

  5. Score anomalies

    Isolation Forest at 5% (high) and 10% (moderate) contamination. Local Outlier Factor gives a second opinion, and a cost-per-m² rule shows what a spreadsheet check would catch.

  6. Human review

    Flags are checked against COA audit findings and news reports where available. A flag starts a question; it does not answer one.

A flag is a statistical signal, not an accusation

A flag means only that a project's cost is unusual compared with similar projects in the dataset. Terrain, right-of-way acquisition, and bridges or drainage bundled into the same contract are common, legitimate reasons for a higher cost.

There are no ground-truth labels for real road projects, so the study treats every flag as a prompt for review. The real dataset will use only public procurement data from PhilGEPS and DPWH.

This sample site runs on synthetic projects with anomalies injected on purpose, so the page can show how many of them the method recovers.
